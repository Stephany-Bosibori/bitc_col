World Wide Web (WWW)

The World Wide Web, often confused with the Internet itself, is actually a service that runs on top of the internet. Proposed by Sir Tim Berners-Lee in 1989, it was designed to allow scientists to share data across different computer systems.

Technical Role

The WWW functions through a system of interlinked hypertext documents accessed via the Internet. It relies on three core technologies: HTML (the language), HTTP (the protocol), and URLs (the addresses).

Significance

It transformed the internet from a command-line utility for researchers into a visual, navigable experience for the general public, enabling the global digital economy we see today.
